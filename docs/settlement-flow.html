🌳 Grove Platform — Settlement Flow

The full life of one order's money: from checkout, to the partner getting paid. Every financial movement is recorded as double-entry ledger postings — immutable, and always balanced (total debits == total credits). Routing, settlement math, and payout aggregation are deterministic code, not AI. Amounts are integer minor units (cents).

Worked example

One delivered order, total $100.00 (10000 cents). Tenant config defines the split:

  • Supplier (goods): $70.00
  • Fulfillment partner (delivery): $20.00
  • Platform fee: $10.00

Where the $100 goes

Supplier $70
Partner $20
Fee $10
Supplier payable Partner payable Platform fee (kept)

End-to-end sequence

Each step names the actor and the records it writes. Steps 5–7 produce the ledger postings shown in the next section.

  1. 1
    Order placedapps/web → API Layer

    Customer checks out. The create-order route is server-authoritative: it recomputes line totals & the order total from DB product prices (never trusts the client), accepts an idempotency key (returns 409 on replay), and runs a stub inventory availability check. If the tenant enables it, checkout is gated on a passing eligibility compliance-check.

    writes → orders (status: pending, paymentStatus: unpaid) · audit-logs (order placed)

  2. 2
    Payment authorizedPaymentProvider adapter

    The selected provider (mock in dev) authorizes the order amount via authorize(). The customer's funds are now committed. On delivery the engine will capture(). Method may be ach / rtp / card / pay-by-bank.

    writes → payments (status: authorized) · updates orders.paymentStatus → authorized, status → confirmed

  3. 3
    Routing selects a partnerRouting Engine (deterministic)

    selectFulfillment(order, candidates) filters partner-locations that are active, have available inventory for every item, and are within serviceRadiusKm of the shipping address; then ranks by distance/speed. It never routes out-of-stock. Inventory is reserved for the order.

    writes → fulfillments (status: assigned) · updates inventory-levels (reserve) · orders.status → routed

  4. 4
    Fulfillment & deliveryPartner Portal / partner ops

    Partner accepts → picks up → in transit → delivered. Each transition is recorded. On delivered, the payment is captured and the order moves toward completion; the reserved inventory is consumed.

    writes → fulfillments.status (accepted…delivered) · payments capture · orders.status → delivered → completed · audit-logs

  5. 5
    Ledger postings (double-entry)Ledger Engine

    When payment captures, the customer's funds enter the ledger. Every posting set shares a transactionId and must balance (Σdebits == Σcredits). Entries are immutable: hooks deny edits/deletes to non-super-admins. See Transaction A below.

    writes → ledger-entries (refType: order) into ledger-accounts

  6. 6
    Settlement allocationSettlement Engine (deterministic)

    On completion, the engine computes the split from tenant config (platform fee %, supplier amount, fulfillment partner amount) and posts balanced ledger entries that move funds from the holding account into each payee's payable account. See Transaction B.

    writes → settlements (allocations[], status: posted) · ledger-entries (refType: settlement)

  7. 7
    Payout batch & disbursementPayout Engine → PayoutProvider

    On the tenant's cadence, the engine aggregates payable balances across orders per payee, creates a payout-batch + one payout per payee, posts ledger entries marking funds settled, then the provider (mock simulates same/next-day) disburses via ACH/RTP. See Transaction C.

    writes → payout-batches · payouts (status: paid) · ledger-entries (refType: payout) · audit-logs

The double-entry postings

A reminder of the rule: in double-entry bookkeeping, every transaction has equal debits and credits. Money is never created or destroyed — it only moves between accounts. Below are the three transactions for our $100 order, in cents. Each one balances.

After payout, the Partner Payable balance for this order is zero — the obligation is settled. The supplier's $70 and the platform's $10 fee follow the same pattern (supplier paid in its own payout; the fee is platform revenue that stays). A refund would post a reversing, balanced transaction (refType: refund) rather than editing any existing entry — the ledger is append-only.
